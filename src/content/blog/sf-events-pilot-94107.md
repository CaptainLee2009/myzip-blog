If you live or work in San Francisco, finding out what is actually happening a few blocks away this week shouldn't take five browser tabs, three calendar apps, and two group chats.

Starting this week, we are running a live **San Francisco Events pilot** in two ZIP codes:
- **SoMa / Mission Bay / Potrero / Dogpatch:** [my.zip/us/94107/events](https://my.zip/us/94107/events)
- **Civic Center / Hayes Valley:** [my.zip/us/94102/events](https://my.zip/us/94102/events)

No account required to browse, no ads, and every card links straight to the organizer's official RSVP or ticket page.

## Why we curate by hand (and why auto-scraping failed)

Early on, we tried the obvious engineering shortcut: hooking up automated ICS calendar feeds and event aggregators to populate timelines automatically.

It broke in every way that makes people stop trusting a local site:
- Venues and street addresses got mangled or truncated into generic city names.
- Recurring spam and off-neighborhood listings flooded the top of the timeline.
- Duplicate cards piled up with broken timestamps and dead links.

A local timeline only works if every item you see is genuinely nearby, accurate, and worth your time. So we threw out the noisy scrapers and switched to **hand-curated Monday and Thursday drops** — written in our own words, verified against the organizer's actual page, and paced calmly so your neighborhood feed stays readable.

## Designed to share in one tap

Most neighborhood plans start when one person drops a link into WhatsApp, Slack, or iMessage. Every event on MyZip now has its own fast, edge-cached page with a clean share card — and tapping **Interested** lets you bookmark the event and set your home ZIP in seconds so you don't miss the next drop.
